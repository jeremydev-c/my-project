## 1. Finalised Problem Statement

> Young people in Nairobi’s high-density neighbourhoods (such as Kibera, Mathare, Pipeline, and Githurai) encounter uncollected waste daily but cannot sort or dispose of it correctly because waste sorting guidance, recycling drop-off locations, and clean-up event information are scattered across adult-facing websites, NGO pages, and WhatsApp groups that no single student knows how to navigate.

---

## 2. Solution Hypothesis

> We believe that secondary school and university students in high-density Nairobi neighbourhoods will be helped by a centralized, student-friendly web application for sorting guidance and drop-off locations because our research shows that despite a desire to act, information is currently scattered across adult-focused platforms that young people do not easily find or navigate.

---

## 3. Research Evidence Summary

First, a Nairobi City County/JICA study revealed that 60% of the city's 3,000 tonnes of daily waste goes uncollected, establishing the high frequency and massive scale of the problem. Second, WRI and Ushahidi flood reports confirmed the severe pain level, showing how uncollected plastic waste clogs drains and directly aggravated deadly flash floods in 2026. Finally, an analysis of existing directories (like Zero Waste Kenya) showed that while recycling hubs exist, their information is scattered across adult-facing platforms, proving that the barrier for students is access to clear, centralized guidance rather than a complete lack of infrastructure.
