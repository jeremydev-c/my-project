## 1. Finalised Problem Statement

> Young people in Nairobi’s high-density neighbourhoods (such as Kibera, Mathare, Pipeline, and Githurai) encounter uncollected waste daily but cannot sort or dispose of it correctly because waste sorting guidance, recycling drop-off locations, and clean-up event information are scattered across adult-facing websites, NGO pages, and WhatsApp groups that no single student knows how to navigate.

## 4. Research Evidence Summary

First, a Nairobi City County/JICA study revealed that 60% of the city's 3,000 tonnes of daily waste goes uncollected, establishing the high frequency and massive scale of the problem. Second, WRI and Ushahidi flood reports confirmed the severe pain level, showing how uncollected plastic waste clogs drains and directly aggravated deadly flash floods in 2026. Finally, a direct interview with a 16-year-old Kibera student named Brian revealed the true barrier: *"I know plastic is bad for the environment, but when I tried to find a recycling bin near me, I just ended up on a bunch of NGO Facebook pages with outdated PDF maps that I couldn't read on my phone"*, proving that the barrier for students is access to clear, centralized, mobile-friendly guidance.
